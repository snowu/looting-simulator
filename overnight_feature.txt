# Overnight balance pass — decision log
# Goal: loot less often, enemies stronger, progression slower, game harder.
# Target feel: King's Field / Shadow Tower. Do not push; leave for the user to test.
# Started 2026-09-13.

## Standing constraints inherited from docs/NEXT.md + memory
- Never invalidate a save. Balance constants are not save data, so retuning is free.
  No SAVE_VERSION bump. Floors already generated in a live run keep their contents.
- Reproducible from the seed. Any NEW roll during generation gets its own hashed
  stream so it cannot reshuffle floors that already exist.
- Punishing is fine; unfair is not. The player must see what got them.
- MECHANICS.md is the single reference for every number.

## D0. Method: measure first, tune second
docs/NEXT.md already specified a headless harness at scripts/playtest.ts as the
first task of the balance pass, and it was never built. Building it first rather
than hand-tuning constants, because the systems interact and every knob I move
shifts three curves I cannot see. Decision: build the harness, take a baseline,
then change one thing at a time against it.

## Baseline measurements (before any change)
Floor composition, per floor, averaged over 60 seeds:
  D1: 10.4 rooms,  9.4 enemies, 4.1 chest, 13.4 urn, 0.9 vault, 0.4 secret, 4.9 loose
  D6:  8.8 rooms, 22.1 enemies, 2.3 chest, 12.7 urn, 0.9 vault, 0.8 secret, 9.9 loose
Loot yield, per floor:
  D1:  181 gold, 40.4 items, 4.1 gear, 1101 gold of value
  D6: 1454 gold, 71.8 items, 10.5 gear, 22447 gold of value
Time-to-kill by gear tier (seconds, average enemy). NOTE: these are
player-optimistic — every enemy is measured at full stamina, on average rolls,
with no dodging and no telegraph read. They are a before/after yardstick, not
a prediction of a real session:
  naked/starter: 2-4s for trash, 12-17s for a Hollow Knight, 86s for the King
  mid (iron Rare): EVERYTHING trash dies in 1 hit (0.78s). King in 17.9s.
  deep (moonsilver Epic): everything except the King dies in ONE hit. King in 8.6s.

## D1. The diagnosis: three separate faults, not one
1. LOOT VOLUME. ~23 lootable containers per floor against a 16-slot backpack.
   40-70 items per floor. There is no scarcity, so there is no decision, so
   there is no King's Field.
2. THE POWER CURVE DIVERGES. Player attack runs 3 -> 6 -> 33 -> 59 across gear
   tiers, a ~20x spread. Enemy HP is a constant (a rat is 10 HP on every floor
   it appears on; depth scaling is only +12% per depth past minDepth and caps
   out after 2-3 floors). The curves are not on the same axis at all.
3. MITIGATION SATURATES. With k=25, a defense of 58 leaves you taking 30% of a
   blow and a defense of 100 reaches the 20% clamp outright. (An earlier draft
   of this note said 58 was already at the clamp; it is not, and the review
   pass was right to catch it. The conclusion is unchanged — late armour
   reduces damage to a rounding error — but the number to tune against is 100.)
   Enemies hit a plated player for 1-7 against 75 HP.
Making enemies "stronger" by raising HP numbers alone would fix none of this: it
would make the early game unwinnable and the late game merely longer.

## D2. The instrument: scripts/playtest.ts + scripts/tables.ts
Built the harness docs/NEXT.md asked for, plus a static table pass that runs in
seconds so it can be re-read after every single knob. `npm run playtest` writes
a report; `vitest run --config vitest.playtest.config.ts scripts/tables.bench.ts`
writes just the tables. Default `npm test` is untouched and still fast.

The table that matters is THE LADDER: for each floor, the enemies native to it,
fought by a player carrying what that floor is supposed to have given them.
Baseline ladder (hits to kill / hits to be killed):
  D1 club+jerkin:      rat 2, goblin 4, skeleton 3  | it needs 10-17 hits. CORRECT.
  D2 copper mace:      almost everything 1-2 hits   | it needs 12-22.
  D3 iron long sword:  almost everything 1 hit      | it needs 18-32.
  D4 iron war axe:     almost everything 1 hit      | it needs 20-26.
  D5 silver plate:     EVERYTHING 1 hit incl. Hollow Knight-2 | it needs 20-37.
  D6 moonsilver:       Ashen King dies in 6 hits    | the final boss needs 13.
D1 is right. The game falls apart on D2 and never recovers.

## D3. Decision: fix the curve, do not just inflate numbers
Player damage grows ~10x from D1 to D6 (6 -> 62) and effective toughness ~4x.
Enemies grow 1.2x. The fix is to put enemies on the same axis, not to multiply
one number. Five levers, in order of how much they do:
  1. Enemy defense is the strongest lever in the game and is barely used.
     mitigate(atk, def, k=15) means def 45 cuts a 62-attack weapon to 15.
     Deep enemies have def 9-12. This is where the armour of the deep goes.
  2. Depth must scale enemy stats on ABSOLUTE depth, not on how far a monster is
     past its own minDepth. The player's gear tracks the floor number; the
     monsters have to track the same thing or the curves cannot meet.
  3. The player's incoming-damage mitigation saturates: floor 0.2 at k=25 means
     plate deletes damage rather than reducing it. Raise k so defense has real
     diminishing returns, and raise the floor so a blow always lands.
  4. Enemy attack has to grow with depth or deep floors cannot threaten.
  5. Base stat blocks for elites and the boss are simply too small.

## D4. Decision: fewer, bigger piles — not just less gold
"Loot too often" is about FREQUENCY, and the economy (market, forge, contracts)
still needs material flowing in. So the target is fewer containers and fewer
items per container, with what is left worth more per pile. Cutting item count
5x and value 5x would starve the town half of the game, which is the half the
README says is the real game.

## D5. Decision: enemy armour is the lever, but a gentle one
First attempt set ENEMY_ARMOUR_K to 22 and scaled armour at 0.8 of the health
curve. That made 30 defense on a Hollow Knight into a 25-swing fight — armour
became a refusal rather than a slope. Walked it back: K=45, armour scaling at
0.45 of the health curve, and the raw defense numbers on the heavies cut by a
third. Armour is now the reason to bring the right weapon, not a wall.

## D6. Decision: three curves from one number
depthPower() now returns the HEALTH multiplier, and attackPower()/defensePower()
express damage and armour as fractions of it (0.55 and 0.45). One curve read
three ways: health climbs fastest, so a deep floor is a longer fight you can
lose slowly rather than a coin flip that removes you in two blows.

## D7. Decision: the stagger threshold had to move with the health scale
`def.hp < 40` sat inline in hitEnemy and decided which monsters get knocked out
of a wind-up. Every stat block is now ~1.5x larger, so that bare 40 would have
silently made the Skeleton Shieldguard stagger-proof. Extracted as STAGGER_HP
and set to 55, which keeps exactly the same creatures on the light side of the
line. This is the class of bug a raw number in the middle of a function causes.

## D8. Corrections from the review pass (overnight_feedback.txt)
Acted on all of it. The substantive ones:
- scripts/ was gitignored wholesale, so the harness would not have been
  committed at all. Now `scripts/*` with negations for the four harness files;
  the personal inject-forge-test-save.js stays untracked as before.
- The harness reported carried loot as "banked". It now settles through the
  real endRun(), so a death takes the pack off you in the numbers too, and
  renown is the number the results screen would show.
- collect() was additive and called from three places, double-counting.
- The parry / no-parry pair used different seed sets, so it measured the seeds.
- The bot emptied piles it could not carry, rewriting the floor to suit itself.
- Taking the stairs only queues a step, so the old code watched for a transition
  that had not started yet and filed three reports for depth 1.

## D9. Decision: re-measure the baseline rather than quote earlier notes
The harness changed a lot while the pass was being written (settlement, stairs
detection, packing potions). A before/after across two different harnesses is
not a before/after. So the baseline was re-run in a `git worktree` at HEAD with
the finished harness copied in, plus a three-line shim giving HEAD the two
functions the harness calls (`attackPower`/`defensePower` as identities, since
HEAD had no such split). Every number in patch_notes.md comes from that pair.

It changed the story. The earlier draft claimed the endgame character reached
3.85 floors before the pass. Measured properly, it reached 6.00 — 24 runs out
of 24, zero deaths, walking out at 70% health with 154 items worth 3603 gold.
That is the thing that needed fixing, and the first draft understated it.

## D10. Decision: leave renown and upgrade costs alone
Difficulty alone slowed progression about fourfold: nobody walks to depth 6 any
more, so nobody collects 14 renown a run any more. Moving the payout AND the
costs on top of that would overshoot, and it is the easiest knob to turn later
once the combat has been felt. Flagged in patch_notes.md as the pair to move if
progression reads as slow rather than pleasantly slow.

## D11. Decision: the Ashen King kills you in three hits, deliberately
At the gear depth 6 is meant to be reached with, he takes ~30 swings and lands
three. That is the tightest number in the game and it assumes you are blocking,
parrying and stepping out of the telegraph — which his 0.8s wind-up, the slowest
in the game, gives you time to do. Flagged for the playtest rather than softened
on a guess, because this is a question about feel and I cannot feel it.

## D12. Two bugs I caused and caught
- A blanket regex trimming every `chance:` in enemies.ts by 30% also hit the
  Ashen King's GUARANTEED drops, turning `chance: 1` into `0.7`. Restored, and
  there is now a test asserting they stay at 1.
- The shrine messages still said "You are whole again" and "You are restored"
  after the font became a 60% mend. Punishing is fine, lying is not.

## Final state

- `npm run build` clean. `npm run playtest` and `npm run tables` both work.
- docs/MECHANICS.md updated throughout, with its monster tables now GENERATED

## D13. Second review pass — three gaps closed, one question answered
Re-checked every point in overnight_feedback.txt against the code rather than
against memory. Most were already done; three were not, and I had counted them
as addressed when they were only partly addressed:

1. TIMEOUTS SETTLED AS EXTRACTIONS. A timed-out run called collect(), which
   settles through endRun(), which banks the pack as a successful delve — then
   only the label was overwritten to 'timeout'. Zero runs time out today so no
   published number is wrong, but it was a live trap. A timeout is now recorded
   unsettled, and summarise() prints a two-line warning telling the reader to
   throw the whole block away.
2. ARRIVAL ACTED ON WHATEVER WAS IN FRONT. navigate() fell through to
   interactionHint(), which answers for any prop, door or staircase the bot
   happens to face — so arriving beside an open door meant closing it every
   tick until the stuck counter fired. Arrival is now only ever about loot;
   stairs are transition()'s job and fights are the adjacent-enemy branch's.
3. GOLD OUT IS STILL NOT MODELLED, and I had quietly let "banked" imply it was.
   The bot never goes to town, so repairs, identification and restocking are
   unpriced. Rather than half-build a town sim, every report block now says so
   in one line. Banked is income, not profit.

Also added the reviewer's own caveat to the generated tables themselves rather
than only to this log: the hit-counts are computed, not played — full stamina,
average rolls, no dodging, blocking or parrying, one monster at a time.

## D14. Depth six is reachable WHEN STOCKED — which is not the same claim
The review agent wrote a prepared-expedition bench to test the thing I had
flagged as unanswerable: is depth 6 reachable by someone who prepares? Endgame
gear, twenty Greater Healing, skip the urns, go down rather than clear floors.
On the final 24-seed report:

  100% of runs reach depth 6. 33% die there — Barrow Champion x3, Hollow Knight
  x2, Flame Wraith, Frost Wisp, Ghoul. About 18 potions per run.

CORRECTION (this entry originally claimed docs/NEXT.md's second success
criterion, "a twentieth run reaches depth 6", was met). It is not, and the
review pass was right to push back. The prepared profile is HANDED its endgame
gear, its renown upgrades and its potions at run start. It establishes that a
well-stocked character can enter depth 6. It says nothing about whether twenty
connected runs of normal play actually earn that loadout, because the harness
plays runs in isolation and never visits town. That remains open, and the
distinction is now stated in both docs/NEXT.md and patch_notes.md.

What it IS good for: the fifth profile in `npm run playtest` is the canary for
the floor multiplier. If it stops reaching depth 6, depthPower has gone too far.

Note on quoting these numbers: earlier figures in this log predate the extra-urn
roll being moved to its own hashed stream and the bot navigation fixes. Quote
the final 24-seed report, not the running commentary above.

## D15. The Ashen King: softened him, then put him back
The reviewer's bench added a `fightBoss` policy (the run harness never actually
fought the King — it reached depth 6 and turned round) and a "King slain" line.
First result: 0 out of 16, with 100% deaths when it tried. Top killer at the
throne was the Hollow Knight, not the King.

I read that as the final encounter being over-tuned and softened him: 470 -> 400
health, 32 -> 26 attack. It changed nothing measurable — still 0/16, and still
three hits to kill you, because 135/58.6 and 135/47.6 both round to 3. So I ran
the experiment I should have run first and gave the bot 60 Greater Healing
instead of 12:

  King slain 12/16. 50% of runs came home. Remaining deaths: Hollow Knight x6.

So the King is beatable and the binding constraint is HEALING SUPPLY ACROSS
DEPTH SIX, not his stat block. The softening was a fix for a misdiagnosis, so it
is reverted — he is 470/32 again. Weakening a final boss on bad evidence is
exactly the change that should not survive.

What is real, and is for the user to judge by feel rather than by bot:
- Depth 6 is a war of attrition on potions. The bot burns 4+ full health bars on
  the floor before the throne room even starts.
- The two Hollow Knight guards kill more often than the King does.
- The bot is a bad proxy for THIS fight specifically: it never steps out of a
  telegraph, and it takes 134 hits in a run where a player would take a fraction
  of that. Its potion count is an upper bound, not an estimate.

## D16. Third review pass
- TRAP DAMAGE ATTRIBUTION was wrong and the reviewer fixed it in place before I
  got there. The `trap` event fires for alarm wards (which do no damage) and for
  traps a monster blundered into, and my flag persisted across frames — so a
  monster hit seconds later could be filed as trap damage. Now batch-local, and
  gated on the trap being under the player and not an alarm. Their version is
  better than the one I was about to write: a local variable makes the batch
  scoping structural instead of depending on a reset call. The corrected split
  turns out to be close to the old one, so the "traps are about a third of
  damage taken in the deep profiles" claim survives on the corrected numbers.
- THE EXTRA URN ROLL was consuming a draw from the floor's shared RNG stream,
  which breaks the project's seed contract (a new roll must not reshuffle the
  rolls after it). The reviewer moved it to its own hashed stream. Correct, and
  I should have caught it: it is the pattern chestIsMimic and shrineKindFor
  already establish, and docs/NEXT.md names it as a standing constraint.
- FIGHT MILK WAS BUYABLE. src/ui/town.ts built the supply shop from every entry
  in CONSUMABLES, so the one found-only Legendary consumable was on the shelf
  for about 504 gold. This predates the balance pass but it defeats exactly the
  scarcity the pass is about. The reviewer filtered it by id in the UI; I moved
  the fix down to SHOP_CONSUMABLES in market.ts, which was the real source of
  truth (exported, unused, and about to become a second leak), and derived the
  exclusion from tonicUnique so the next tonic added is excluded for free. Test
  added.

## D17. The Warden tree, measured rather than guessed
User feedback after playing: difficulty reads much better, and the Warden (the
renown upgrade tree) now needs the same treatment. Correct — D10 deferred it on
purpose, and the tree was still priced for a game where nothing could kill you
and the floor handed you forty items.

Built scripts/upgrades.bench.ts: same seeds, each upgrade maxed and ALONE,
against no upgrades at all. Two things it taught me, both of which I would have
got wrong by reasoning:

1. PACK MULE MEASURED AT EXACTLY ZERO — zero depth, zero survival, zero haul.
   Not noise: with the 16-slot pack and the new loot rates, NO scripted profile
   at any depth ever filled a pack. Peak usage was 5 to 19 slots against 16 to
   28. So the loot cut had silently deleted an upgrade, and with it the "what do
   I carry home" decision, in a game called Looting Simulator. Fixed at the
   source: BASE_BACKPACK 16 -> 12. Pack Mule now measures +85g a run.
2. MY FIRST COMPOSITE SCORE RANKED SUPPLY CRATE WORST IN THE TREE. It mixed
   depth, survival and haul into one "worth" number — and potions take the bot
   deeper, and deeper is where it dies, so the survival term punished the single
   most valuable upgrade in the game. I nearly repriced on it. Deleted the
   composite, kept raw deltas, and wrote the confound into the bench output so
   the next reader distrusts that column too.

## D18. Decision: do not reprice what the harness cannot see
Five of eleven upgrades are invisible to this bot. Silver Tongue, Market
Insider, Master Smith and Appraiser's Eye act entirely in town, and the bot
never goes to town. Second Wind measures zero as well, but for a different
reason worth writing down: at 22/s regen and a ~1.1s axe cycle, regeneration
roughly matches cost per swing, so stamina does not bind for a slow weapon. It
does bind for fast ones, which the bot does not carry.

All five are left alone. The bench prints them as UNMEASURED rather than as
zero, because a zero there is a statement about the harness and repricing on it
would be exactly the mistake the composite score nearly caused.

## D19. Treasure Sense: buffed the effect, then put the price back
Raised +12% -> +20% per level AND raised the cost, which is incoherent — I made
it stronger and more expensive in the same edit. Checked the effect analytically
instead (yields() over 60 deterministic seeds, far more sensitive than 24 runs):
+60 find is worth +15% gear and +5% loot value, because the Common band's flat
weight of 100 anchors the whole roll. So it is a modest upgrade by construction.
Kept the +20, put the cost back to 5/10/16.

## D20. The save risk in a smaller backpack
Shrinking BASE_BACKPACK is the one change in this whole pass that could eat a
player's items: an older save has a town loadout sized for sixteen slots. It
cannot — addItem refuses to overfill rather than dropping, and startRun returns
the overflow to the unlimited stash. Verified and pinned with a test, because
"never wipe a save" is the rule this project runs on and I would rather it fail
in CI than in someone's browser.

Tree total 323 against 314: the shape moved, the total did not.

## D21. Treasure Sense: the user was right, for a reason the numbers hid
User: "treasure sense should cost more, 20% loot is a whole lot." My measurement
said the opposite — +60 find was only +15% gear and +5% value, so I had argued
it should stay cheap.

Both were true, and the gap between them was the bug. Find multiplied the good
rarity bands but Common's weight was a flat 100, so it anchored the roll: a
maxed Treasure Sense bought about 15% MORE DROPS OF WHICH NEARLY ALL WERE STILL
COMMON. More junk, not better junk. That is not what "loot find" means to anyone
reading it, and it is why the label felt like a whole lot while the numbers said
it was nothing. The user was reading the promise; I was measuring the delivery.

Fixed the delivery rather than the promise:
- Common's weight is now 100/f as well as the good bands being x f, so find
  moves quality, not only quantity. At find 0 it is exactly 100, so nothing
  changes for a player who has bought none.
- Find now also scales a chest's valuable and gem rolls, which it never touched
  — a chalice or a sunstone is precisely what a treasure sense should surface.
Result at +60 find: +22% gear pieces and +85% more Uncommons (was +15%/+0%).
Then raised the cost as asked: 5/10/16 -> 6/13/22. Tree total 333.

Note for the record: the 24-run bench STILL reports treasure_sense at about
zero, because a single Epic drop swings banked value by hundreds of gold and
24 runs cannot resolve a ten-percent effect. The analytical table over 60
deterministic seeds can. The bench now says so in its own output rather than
printing a number that contradicts the table.
